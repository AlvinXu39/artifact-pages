The shape of it, before the detail

Background

This is a logistics-risk event, not a tariff change. When vessel traffic falls on chokepoint routes, carriers adjust routing, insurance, schedules and blank-sailing choices. The commercial impact reaches factories through longer calendars and more uncertain freight booking (LIVE).

What's being affected

The most exposed orders are time-sensitive goods moving through Middle East chokepoints or linked transshipment networks. Seasonal apparel, home textiles and buyer-specific replenishment feel delay risk faster than loose-window cargo (HYPOTHESIS).

Likely impact

The disruption is enough to reopen delivery-buffer and freight-cost conversations. It does not automatically move sourcing, but it can delay PO timing, extend cash cycles and make buyers compare suppliers with stronger route visibility.

The split is route exposure, time sensitivity and cash tolerance.

Freight risk path

Freight disruption rarely hits every supplier equally. The same route shock can be a late-delivery problem, a freight-cost problem or a cash-cycle problem.

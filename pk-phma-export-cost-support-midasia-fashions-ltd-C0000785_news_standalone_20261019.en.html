No effective date or end date was stated.

The shape of it, before the detail

Background

PHMA raised the issue in a Ministry of Commerce consultation after higher electricity, gas, labour, compliance and levy costs squeezed exporters. The request is not a tariff change; it is a cost and working-capital signal that suppliers may struggle to hold prices and delivery promises.

What's being affected

The source names hosiery, knitwear and apparel exports, plus ocean freight that rose 1.5 to 2.5 times on some routes. The exposed group is Pakistan-based exporters carrying those costs; the comparison group is regional suppliers whose buyer quotes are not being reset by the same local burden.

Likely impact

The 6–8% FOB support ask shows the size of the relief exporters say they need to protect orders. The gap can reopen price talks immediately, but it does not move an order by itself unless reliability, refund delay or financing stress appears together.

The factory question is whether this is your own margin pressure, a competitor opening, or only a buyer-pricing signal.

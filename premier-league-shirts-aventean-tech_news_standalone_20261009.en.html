Football shirts hit scrutiny. Cambodia factories named. Buyers will ask harder questions.

BBC Sport traced official football shirts for major clubs to two Cambodian Adidas suppliers, Bowker Garment Factory and Trax Apparel. Factories named in the investigation face immediate scrutiny; other apparel suppliers face buyer requests for wage, heat, grievance and safety proof. The story was published on 8 Oct 2026.

The shape of it, before the detail

Background

BBC Sport reported that workers making official club shirts in Cambodia were paid basic wages of less than £1 an hour and described heat, verbal abuse and electrical-safety concerns (verified). Adidas said its standards and audits are intended to protect workers. The point is not a new law; it is a public compliance signal that forces brands to re-check factory evidence.

What's being affected

The named sites are Bowker Garment Factory and Trax Apparel in Cambodia, with products linked to clubs including Arsenal, Celtic, Liverpool and Real Madrid (verified). The commercial scope is broader: any buyer handling licensed sportswear, club merchandise or high-visibility apparel will ask whether similar labour, heat, grievance and electrical-safety risks exist in its approved factory base.

Likely impact

This does not raise duty or freight cost. It raises compliance proof pressure before repeat orders. Buyers are unlikely to move a complex licensed programme overnight, but they can freeze expansion, request corrective evidence, shift replenishment to approved alternatives, or delay onboarding until site-level remediation is documented.

Below is where the pressure lands, how it changes your position, and what to prepare before the buyer asks.

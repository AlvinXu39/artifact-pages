Sep 2026 Supply Policy Bangladesh Import Policy Order — Knit Fabric Affects you

Bangladesh bans knit fabric imports. Domestic mills are now the only option. MMF and sportswear need association certification.

Bangladesh's new Import Policy Order blocks all standard knit fabric imports to protect domestic mill investment. Standard cotton and commodity knit fabrics — blocked at the border; specialized MMF, sportswear, and functional knit can still be imported but require an industry association recommendation and lien bank certification. BGMEA and BKMEA have demanded withdrawal, but the order is in force now.

The shape of it, before the detail

Background

The government restricted knit fabric imports after domestic mills argued that overseas supply was undercutting their capital investments. The ban covers all commodity knit fabric categories — cotton jersey, interlock, rib — with no grace period for shipments already in transit without the new documentation. Industry associations have pushed back publicly but the order stands.

What's being affected

All standard knit fabric is now blocked for import into Bangladesh. The exemption applies only to specialized MMF, sportswear fabrics, and functional textiles, and requires both an association recommendation letter and lien bank certification before the goods ship. Woven fabrics — HS 52–55 — are not covered; only knit (HS 60) is affected.

Likely impact

For knitwear factories that sourced specialty fabric from Korea, China, or Taiwan, the domestic mill alternative covers commodity cotton knit adequately but lacks scale and range for technical or performance fabrics. Factories that need the exemption path face a 2-step documentation process before each import. The uncertainty is acute now because associations have not confirmed processing timelines.

The policy draws two clear lines — what is completely blocked and what is still importable with compliance steps — and puts the documentation burden on the factory, not the buyer.

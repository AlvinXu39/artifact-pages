The shape of it, before the detail

Background

This is a working-capital and industrial-stability event, not a tariff change. The pressure comes from delayed incentives, shorter production days, weaker demand, lower prices, higher costs and the need to pay wages and Eid bonuses on time.

What's being affected

The exposure sits in Bangladesh RMG factories that rely on pending cash incentives, wage-support loans, packing credit or pre-shipment credit to bridge payroll and shipment cycles. Non-Bangladesh suppliers feel it through buyer reliability checks.

Likely impact

Tk 5,700 crore of pending incentives is large enough to change short-term cash planning. Buyers may not move programs immediately, but they will ask whether payroll, production days and shipment financing can hold through Eid.

The factory question is whether the event is your own payroll risk, a competitor opening, or a buyer-financing signal.

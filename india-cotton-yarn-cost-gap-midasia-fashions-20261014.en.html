14 Sep 2026 Until 31 Oct 2026 Raw material cost India textiles Cotton import duty Affects you

Cotton duty may return. Only imported cotton is exposed. The yarn gap stays small.

India's textile industry asked the government to extend duty-free raw cotton imports for 6 months beyond 31 Oct. Indian mills using imported cotton lose that relief if the waiver ends; mills using domestic Indian cotton do not. No extension decision was announced.

The shape of it, before the detail

Background

India waived the cotton import duty from 1 Jun to 31 Oct 2026. The Cotton Association of India estimates 2025-26 imports at 62 lakh bales against consumption of 348 lakh bales, about 18% of mill use. The other roughly 82% is domestic cotton.

What's being affected

The duty sits on raw cotton, not on a finished garment. Cotton feeds yarn, and yarn feeds the garment. A factory that buys yarn feels the move only through the yarn invoice, and only for the cotton share inside that yarn.

Likely impact

If the waiver ends, the 11% levy returns on imported cotton only. Applied to an 18% import share, that is about 2% of the cotton pile, and less than that in yarn and in FOB. It can reopen a comparison. It is not large enough by itself to move an order.

The cost path is cotton, then yarn, then the garment.

Yarn cost path

A garment factory does not buy the cotton bale. It buys yarn. The duty can change yarn cost only when the cotton inside that yarn was imported into India.

You are a Bangladesh woven FOB factory mapped to COMBO-002. Your raw material is yarn. Cotton is the raw material inside that yarn. Bangladesh does not grow cotton, so local yarn is not duty-free domestic cotton. It is yarn spun from imported cotton.

Industry data still puts about 60% of Bangladesh yarn supply on India. That figure is not your invoice. If your woven programmes use Indian yarn, your own yarn cost follows the Indian spinner. You do not stand apart from the competitor on that portion.

The competitive slice is narrower. Only about 18% of Indian cotton is imported. An 11% levy on that slice is about 2% of the cotton used by Indian mills, and a still smaller share of yarn and of FOB.

The impact is competitive and limited, and the potential advantage against Indian yarn is small. It exists only if the waiver is refused. No extension has been approved, and until 31 Oct imported cotton in India is still the cheaper case.

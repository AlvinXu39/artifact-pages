Until 31 Oct 2026 Raw material cost India textiles Cotton import duty Affects you

Cotton duty relief may stay. Yarn pressure remains. Buyers will ask for proof.

India's textile industry asked the government to extend duty-free raw cotton imports for 6 months beyond 31 Oct. Factories exposed to cotton yarn buying need relief from high input cost; farmers selling the new crop worry imports could suppress local prices. No extension decision was announced.

The shape of it, before the detail

Background

India waived the 11% import duty on raw cotton from June to 31 Oct to increase cotton availability and tame domestic prices (LIVE). Export groups now want the duty-free window extended because yarn prices have moved faster than apparel prices.

What's being affected

The direct exposure is raw cotton, cotton yarn, knitwear, towels and other cotton-rich textile programmes. Open yarn positions feel the pressure fastest; synthetic goods or already-funded inventory are less exposed to this specific duty decision.

Likely impact

The article cited cotton yarn prices up 60% in 2026 and yarn at about 60% of garment raw-material cost (LIVE). That is large enough to reopen costing talks, but not enough by itself to move orders unless buyers see evidence by programme.

The pressure splits by cotton exposure, policy timing and cash capacity.

Material cost path

This is a cotton input and cash-cycle event. It does not hit every supplier equally; exposure depends on product fibre, pricing status and who funds yarn before shipment.

You are a Bangladesh woven FOB factory mapped to COMBO-002. This Indian cotton-duty request does not change your export tariff. The commercial question is yarn supply and buyer allocation, not a duty cut on your FOB.

Industry data for this COMBO says Bangladesh still takes a large share of yarn from India (PROXY, about 60%). If you buy yarn yourself, an Indian duty return after 31 Oct can raise input cost with a lag. If buyers nominate yarn, the same move hits them first and you feel it as allocation pressure, not a factory duty change. The impact is indirect and scenario-dependent; no extension has been approved.

Keep two books: your India-linked yarn exposure, and the Indian exporters now asking buyers to wait for cheaper cotton. Buyers will compare those two stories, not a generic yarn complaint.

Watch open woven programmes with long shipment windows and any India-origin yarn quotes. Those files decide whether this stays a conversation or becomes a cost claim.

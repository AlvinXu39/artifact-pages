September 2026 Supply Disruption India India Cotton Shortage Affects you

India’s cotton ran short. Tiruppur just got expensive. Bangladesh didn’t move.

India’s cotton shortage drove yarn up 60% since early 2026, adding ~15% to production costs at Tiruppur’s knit exporters. Factories shipping from India face that cost surge plus a 9.6% EU tariff; factories shipping from Bangladesh pay zero EU duty on the same garments. Both gaps are live as of September 2026.

The shape of it, before the detail

Background

India’s 2025–26 cotton crop fell roughly 10 million bales short of domestic demand. Textile mills across Tiruppur — India’s primary knit export hub — compete for limited cotton on the spot and short-term contract market. Yarn prices moved from Rs 250/kg in January 2026 to Rs 400/kg by September: a 60% rise in under nine months. The shortage is crop-cycle structural and cannot be resolved by policy.

What’s being affected

The cost surge hits HS 61 knit garments — t-shirts, polo shirts, sweatshirts, leggings — where cotton yarn is 50–70% of production cost. Tiruppur exporters buying on spot contracts absorb the full increase immediately. Bangladesh knitwear factories source ~60% of their yarn from India; factories on buyer-nominated yarn structures pass the input cost shock to the buyer’s side rather than the factory.

Likely impact

In the EU, India-origin knit garments carry a 9.6% tariff while Bangladesh-origin garments pay zero under EBA. Combined with the 15% production cost headwind, the total India-to-Bangladesh EU landed cost gap is now 20–25% wider than at the start of 2026 (verified from TARIFF-DB). A gap this size is large enough to redirect EU programme allocations — not just reopen conversations — especially for standard basics where qualification time is short.

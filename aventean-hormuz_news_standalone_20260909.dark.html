The shape of it, before the detail

Background

Iran has reduced Hormuz tanker transits while signalling a formal restricted-zone declaration, which would force carriers to reroute around the Cape of Good Hope, adding 12–14 days and roughly $300–500 per container. The Cape bypass is already the default for carriers avoiding Houthi attacks in the Red Sea, so available vessel bandwidth on that lane is constrained entering Q4.

What's being affected

Asia-Europe lanes via the Indian Ocean and Suez Canal face the highest exposure — tanker count through Hormuz is down 33%. Transpacific routes are less directly affected but share vessel capacity with the Indian Ocean corridor. Q4 holiday goods — clothing, electronics, hardgoods — are the most time-sensitive cargo at risk right now.

Likely impact

Standard Suez routing allows 25–28 days factory-to-port for Asia-Europe lanes. Cape rerouting pushes that to 37–42 days, moving October shipments into late-November delivery — after most retail holiday floor-sets are locked. The extra 12–14 days is enough to disrupt a seasonal programme, not just add a surcharge.

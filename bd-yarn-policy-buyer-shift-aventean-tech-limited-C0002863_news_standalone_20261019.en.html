The shape of it, before the detail

Background

The article ties several Bangladesh policy moves to buyer concern: land-port yarn import restrictions, bonded warehouse changes, safeguard-duty proposals and import-policy reversals. This is a policy-stability and raw-material-access event, not a new tariff paid at the buyer border.

What's being affected

The strongest exposure is knitwear and garment production using 10–30 count cotton yarn, especially where raw material access depends on bank-guarantee procedures. Inditex, H&M and C&A were named as buyers watching cost, lead time and policy reliability.

Likely impact

The quoted local yarn move from $3.15 to as high as $3.70 per kg is enough to reopen costing and allocation decisions. Buyers may not switch every order now, but they can hold volume, compare Pakistan or Cambodia, and demand proof of stable inputs.

The factory question is whether buyer concern creates a direct raw-material risk or a competitive sourcing opening.

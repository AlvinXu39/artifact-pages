US Section 301 splits its import tax in two. Vietnam went up. Bangladesh didn’t move.

The US now charges an extra import tax on goods from 60 countries. Countries with a forced-labour import law pay 10%. Those without pay 12.5%. Vietnam and China moved up. Bangladesh, India, and Cambodia stayed put. That 2.5% gap is now live — and your buyers have noticed. This took effect 24 July 2026 (VERIFIED).

The shape of it, before the detail

Background

The USTR added an extra import tax on goods from 60 countries under Section 301, on top of existing tariffs. Countries with a law banning forced-labour imports pay 10%. Those without pay 12.5%. It went live 24 July 2026, collected when goods cross the US border. A 25-state legal challenge was filed 3 August 2026 — rates remain active until any court injunction (VERIFIED).

What’s being affected

Almost all goods the US imports — apparel, home goods, toys, hardgoods. The short exempt list (steel, aluminium, cars, medicines) already pays Section 232 duties, not this one. 17 countries pay 10%, including Bangladesh, India, Cambodia. 43 pay 12.5%, including Vietnam and China (VERIFIED).

Likely impact

Until now, all 60 countries paid the same rate. The split creates a 2.5% cost gap between the 2 groups (VERIFIED). Not enough to move an order on its own — qualification cost, approved-supplier status, and delivery risk usually outweigh a narrow gap — but enough for buyers to reopen conversations they had already settled.

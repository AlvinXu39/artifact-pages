Sep 2026 Trade Pricing Bangladesh · US US Reciprocal Tariff Affects you

US tariffs drove Bangladesh apparel prices down. Buyer discount demands have now eased. The repricing window is open — briefly.

US reciprocal tariffs pushed Bangladesh exporters to absorb up to half the duty burden, cutting average FOB unit prices 2.26% in Jan–Jul 2026. Factories shipping to US buyers bore the full squeeze — export value fell 6.5% and volume 4.34%; EU-market factories were not subject to this buyer pricing pressure. The tariff has since dropped from 20% to 10%, and buyer discount demands have eased.

The shape of it, before the detail

Background

The US imposed reciprocal tariffs on Bangladesh apparel starting at 10% (April 2025), raised to 20% for part of Q1 2026, then reduced to 10% in April 2026 following a court ruling. Buyers used the elevated tariff as leverage, requiring exporters to absorb one-third to one-half of the additional duty — a direct cut in FOB unit prices. US OTEXA data cited by Bangladesh Apparel Voice confirms the 2.26% average unit-price decline across the seven-month period (VERIFIED).

What's being affected

Bangladesh apparel exports to the US — knit (HS 61) and woven (HS 62) — were directly in scope. EU export programmes carry zero duty under EBA and were not subject to US buyer pressure. FOB-priced contracts where the factory absorbed tariff risk took the full price compression; CIF or DDP contracts where the buyer paid at the border were shielded.

Likely impact

A 2.26% unit price cut on CMT margins of 5–12% wipes out 1–2 percentage points of gross margin — enough to turn a breakeven order into a loss at the lower margin band. The tariff reduction from 20% to 10% removes buyers' stated justification for the discount. The window to reprice upward is short: buyers will lock next-season contracts at current compressed rates unless factories act first.

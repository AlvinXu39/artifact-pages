July 19, 2026 Trade Regulation European Union ESPR · Reg 2024/1781 Affects you

EU banned brands from destroying unsold clothes. Large retailers must find another way. Expect smaller, tighter orders.

The European Commission prohibited large fashion brands from destroying unsold apparel, clothing accessories, and footwear, effective 19 July 2026. Companies with 250 or more employees and annual turnover above €50M must now donate, resell, or recycle excess stock; medium-sized companies follow in 2030. The release valve brands used for over-ordering is now closed.

The shape of it, before the detail

Background

The EU’s Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR, Reg 2024/1781) targets a practice widespread in fashion: large brands routinely incinerated or shredded unsold inventory to protect exclusivity and avoid discount channels. The European Commission adopted the implementing delegated act on 9 February 2026, closing that option and defining narrow exceptions that each require five years of documentation.

What’s being affected

The ban covers all unsold finished apparel, clothing accessories, and footwear — not raw fabric or yarn. Large companies (250+ employees, €50M+ turnover) are banned from 19 July 2026. Medium companies (50–249 employees) face the same ban from 2030. Small businesses are exempt indefinitely. A handful of documented exceptions apply — safety non-compliance, hygiene issues — but each requires formal justification kept on file.

Likely impact

Until now, large brands used destruction as a cheap inventory exit — letting them over-order freely against forecast uncertainty. The ban removes that exit. Brands now carry a real cost for every unit they cannot sell: storage, donation logistics, or resale platforms. This is not a gap large enough to shift sourcing between countries. It is a structural change in how buyers manage PO quantities — buyers will order closer to actual demand, cutting the seasonal over-order buffers that were part of your volume.

Sep 8, 2026 Trade Policy Canada Canada Counter-Tariff — C$27.6B Affects you

Canada hit US goods with a 25% counter-tariff. Sports gear, clothing, furniture covered. Bangladesh shippers are not affected.

Canada imposed counter-tariffs effective September 8, 2026, across C$27.6 billion of US imports, matching US Section 338 tariff levels dollar-for-dollar. US-manufactured goods — including sporting equipment, clothing, and furniture — now pay 25% at the Canadian border; goods shipped directly from non-US origins, including Bangladesh, face no change to their Canadian import duties. Steel and aluminium from the US rise from 25% to 50%.

The shape of it, before the detail

Background

Canada's counter-tariff is a direct response to US Section 338, targeting 600-plus US product classifications chosen to mirror US tariff pressure. The tariff applies at origin: goods manufactured in the US pay 25% even if assembled from imported components; goods shipped directly from Bangladesh, Vietnam, or India pay standard Canadian import duties — unchanged by this action.

What's being affected

Furniture, clothing, and sports equipment lead the 600-plus covered categories, alongside household goods and consumer electronics. Steel and aluminium from the US jump from 25% to 50%. Goods from Canada's other trade partners — including Bangladesh, Vietnam, and India — face no change to their Canadian market-access rates under this order.

Likely impact

US factories had a 0% cost advantage into Canada under CUSMA. The 25% counter-tariff eliminates that advantage and creates the reverse: Bangladesh-origin goods are now 20–25% cheaper than US-made equivalents at the Canadian border. That gap is wide enough to redirect sourcing decisions, not just reopen conversations — qualification for standard clothing and sporting goods typically runs 1 season or less.

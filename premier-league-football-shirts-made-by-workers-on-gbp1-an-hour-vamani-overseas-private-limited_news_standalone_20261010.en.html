Football shirts hit scrutiny. Cambodia factories named. Buyers will ask harder questions.

BBC Sport traced official football shirts for major clubs to two Cambodian Adidas suppliers. Factories named in the investigation face immediate scrutiny; other apparel suppliers face buyer requests for wage, heat, grievance and safety proof. The story is live now.

The shape of it, before the detail

Background

BBC Sport reported that workers making official club shirts in Cambodia were paid basic wages of less than £1 an hour and described heat, verbal-abuse and electrical-safety concerns (VERIFIED). Adidas said its standards and audits are intended to protect workers. This is not a new law; it is a public compliance signal that forces brands to re-check factory evidence.

What's being affected

The named sites are Bowker Garment Factory and Trax Apparel in Cambodia, with products linked to major football clubs. The commercial scope is broader: licensed sportswear and high-visibility apparel buyers will ask whether similar labour, heat, grievance and electrical-safety risks exist in their approved factory base (VERIFIED).

Likely impact

This does not raise duty or freight cost. It raises compliance proof pressure before repeat orders. Buyers are unlikely to move complex licensed programmes overnight, but they can freeze expansion, request corrective evidence, shift replenishment to approved alternatives, or delay onboarding.

The pressure lands by site exposure, evidence quality and product visibility.

Which factories, which evidence, which orders

The event splits suppliers by site exposure, by how clean their social-compliance evidence is, and by how visible the product is to consumers and fans.

US freight is squeezing Bangladesh. Buyers resist price. Cash gets tighter.

Bangladesh exporters warned that high US freight, raw-material inflation and expensive bank financing are weakening competitiveness. Chattogram-to-New York garment containers were quoted up to $11,800 for 20-foot and $12,900 for 40-foot; Europe-bound lanes were much lower at $3,000 and $4,586. No legal effective date applies.

The shape of it, before the detail

Background

Exporters say US freight has stayed high while raw-material prices and bank financing costs are also rising. The pressure is commercial, not a statute: the bill arrives through carrier quotes, working capital and buyer negotiation.

What's being affected

The named lane is Chattogram to New York, with Europe used as the comparison. US-bound apparel is most exposed; raw-material imports also feel it when higher inbound cost lands before buyer payment.

Likely impact

A 40-foot US quote at $12,900 versus $4,586 to major European ports creates a cost gap big enough to reopen pricing talks. It can delay orders even when the factory remains approved.

The split is by shipping lane, cost pass-through and cash capacity.

Freight-to-cash path

This is a freight and working-capital event. It does not hit every supplier equally; exposure depends on shipping lane, price terms and who funds raw material before shipment.
